Combat Rating Bench

Teams of Combatants fight each other in the engine, and a candidate Combat rating formula predicts who wins. A good formula gives two teams of equal rating a 50% win rate whatever their make-up, and a win rate that follows the ratio of the ratings the same way for every pair. The Combats do not read the formula, so editing it re-scores the Combats already fought at once, and Fit searches the checked parameters for the best formula.

Formula

Each group of identical Combatants rates by the combatant expression; a team rates by the team expression over the sum of its Combatants' ratings. A Combatant that is not able to fight at the start counts for nothing. The expressions are JavaScript, with Math in scope, e.g. sqrt(health).

Combatant variables: skill (effective skill), base_skill, health, armor, damage, exhaustion, and the derived health_mult = 1 + (health − health_base) × health_per_point, likewise armor_mult and damage_mult. Team variables: sum, count (Combatants able to fight), and game (the game's base Combat rating, Σ effective skill). Every parameter is a variable of both.

Teams

Each team is a list of groups of identical Combatants at full health. Sizes multiplies every group's count, giving one team per entry, e.g. 4, 8, 16; each such team is called a variant below.

Experiment

The engine is not symmetric: the Agents' side acts first in every Combat round, picks its targets, and alone can retreat. With Both sides, every pair fights twice, each team once as the Agents' side, and a team's win rate is the mean of the two, so that neither side's edge leaks into the result.

With Size every team, each team other than the first fights in one variant only, whose first group's count gives the rating closest to the first variant's, so every pair should be a 50% fight. Retreat is off by default, because it reads the game's own Combat rating, not this formula, and a retreat counts as a loss.

Results

The prediction is P(A wins) = r^k / (1 + r^k) with r = team_cr(A) / team_cr(B), which is exactly 50% at equal ratings; k is fitted to the Combats, so it measures how sharply the win rate turns, and the error measures how well the formula orders the teams.
