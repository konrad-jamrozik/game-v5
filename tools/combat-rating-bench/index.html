Combat Rating Bench

Every Mission site's Enemies fight Agent teams sized by a candidate Combat rating formula to a set share of the site's rating, e.g. 100%, 120%, and 150%. A good formula gives the teams sized to 100% a 50% win rate whatever their skill and Upgrades, and the teams sized to each larger share one consistent win rate. Nothing is fought until you press a Run button; results are kept in the browser, and a cell whose team the current formula sizes differently shows its earlier result greyed until it is run again.

Formula

Each group of identical Combatants rates by the combatant expression; a team rates by the team expression over the sum of its Combatants' ratings. A Combatant that is not able to fight counts for nothing. The expressions are JavaScript, with Math in scope, e.g. sqrt(health).

Combatant types under the formula

combatant_cr is one Combatant's rating by the combatant expression; × skill is that rating over its effective skill, i.e. how much its health, armor, and weapon damage raise or lower it. A team's rating then comes from the team expression over the sum of these, so with a team expression other than sum, e.g. sqrt(sum * count), a site's rating is on another scale than its Combatants'.

Every parameter applies at its value, whether or not its Fit box is checked; e.g. 0 switches a CR per point off. The box only lets the Fit button change the parameter. Fit fights nothing: over the cells already fought for the current formula, it searches the checked parameters for the values whose ratings best predict the measured win rates, by the curve under Analysis, which is exactly 50% at equal ratings, and writes them into the table. The unchecked parameters stay as they are. The new values usually size many teams differently, so those cells turn grey until they are run again; run, fit, and run the grey cells until the values settle.

Combatant variables: skill (effective skill), base_skill, health, armor, damage, exhaustion, agent (1 for an Agent, 0 for an Enemy), and the derived health_mult = 1 + (health − health_base) × cr_per_health, likewise armor_mult and damage_mult. Team variables: sum, count (Combatants able to fight), and game (the game's base Combat rating, Σ effective skill). Every parameter, and every Combat value under Setup, e.g. rating_bonus_per_weapon_damage_upgrade, is a variable of both.

Setup

Agent teams are Agents of the AgentArchetype's health, weapon damage, and armor, at full health and rested, with the given base skill and Upgrades; each Weapon damage or Armor Upgrade adds its increment to every Agent. Each team takes the Agent count whose rating is closest to its share of the Mission site's rating. The Agent team is always the Agents' side, as in a Mission.

Retreat is off by default, by setting retreat_below_agent_rating_fraction to 0, because it reads the game's own Combat rating rather than this formula; when it is allowed, a retreat counts as a loss. A cell whose closest Agent count still misses its share by more than 5% is excluded: drawn dashed, never fought, and left out of the statistics and the fit, e.g. one Agent of skill 500 against a small site.

Mission sites

Each cell is one Agent team against the row's Mission site: its Agent count above, its win rate over the Combats below, tinted red when the Agents win less than 50% and green when they win more; an excluded cell shows excl. Hover a cell for its ratings. Run beside a share fights that block, Run row the whole row, and Run in the top row that share for every site.

Analysis

Over the cells with a result for the current formula, all within 5% of their share. The spread is the standard deviation of the win rates of one share; the dice alone, i.e. a finite number of Combats, leave the spread stated beside it, and the rest is the formula spread, the part a better formula can remove.

Win rates of each share

Win rate by rating ratio

The curve is P = r^k / (1 + r^k) with r the Agent team's rating over the site's, exactly 50% at equal ratings, with k fitted to the cells; Fit searches the checked parameters for the least squared error of this curve.
