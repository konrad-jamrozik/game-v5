CR Sweep Bench

Finds a Combat rating formula from controlled experiments. One Combatant, the reference, is the yardstick: the strength of a team is the number of reference Combatants it is an even match for, measured by the game's own engine. Changing one value of a team at a time shows what that value alone is worth, and a formula is scored by how close it comes to those measurements.

Starting the engine…

1. The experiments

The yardstick and the values tried

s is skill, h max health, d weapon damage, and a armor. Each value is tried on a team of the team size whose every Combatant is the reference with that one value changed. Every team is measured twice, once as the Agents' side and once as the Enemies' side, because Agents strike first and choose their targets. Everyone is at full health and rested, and no one runs out of Ammo.

Other Combatant types

One per line, e.g. elite: s400 h30 d10 a0. Each is measured at every team size, mixed half and half with every other type, and sent as Agents against Enemy teams of every type and against the Mission sites.

Rules

GDR values to fight with in place of the content's, one per line, e.g. exhaustion_per_defense = 0. Measurements under other rules are swept and kept apart.

The values in force

Sweep

A measurement fixes one side and sweeps the count of one Combatant type on the other, to find the count at which the Agents win half the Combats and the count at which they win 95%.

2. What one value is worth

Each chart changes one thing and holds everything else at the reference. The dots are measured strengths, in reference Combatants; the lines are what the formula of section 5 predicts. Where a line leaves the dots, the formula misjudges that value.

3. Whether the values combine simply

Two values changed together

If two values acted independently, changing both would multiply their two effects. A gap of 100% means they do; a gap away from it means the formula needs a term that ties the two together.

Two types in one team

If strength were a plain sum over the Combatants, a team of two types would be worth its two halves together. A gap of 100% means it is.

4. Whether one number can work, and what the dice cost

Fights the yardstick did not see

Agents of one type against an Enemy team that is not the reference. The yardstick says how many reference Combatants each side is worth, and so how many Agents make an even match. If one number per side could describe a team, every dot would lie on the line. The spread is the least error any formula can have.

The dice

How many more reference Agents win 95% of the Combats than win half of them, against the same Enemies. No formula changes this: it is how far the safe ratio must lie above even odds.

What an expression may read

A Combatant's expression reads skill, damage, health, armor, and count, the number of Combatants of its side. A side's reads sum, sum2, and count. Both read the parameters, the functions sqrt min max pow log exp abs ceil floor round, and the means of the other side, opp_count, opp_skill, opp_damage, opp_health, and opp_armor. A formula that reads the other side rates one team differently against each opponent, so use those only to find out what a formula misses.

Parameters

Fit brings the rating ratio at even odds as close to 100% as it can over the experiments. It never reads the Mission sites, which only examine the result.
